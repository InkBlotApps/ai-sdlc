Vocabulary & taxonomy

Organization Adoption

Adoption layer

Standardize the meaning, not necessarily the words.

Organizations should be able to keep the vocabulary their developers, architects, operators, auditors, and business teams already use while mapping it onto stable canonical concepts.

Canonical modelApplication · Team · Control · Exception · Incident

Names are presentation. IDs are identity.

An organization may rename “Application” to “Digital Product” without breaking evidence, APIs, automation, policy mappings, or history.

Multiple taxonomies can coexist

The same Batch may be an Application to engineering, a Workload to security, a Business Application to architecture, and a Cost Object to finance.

Classification should drive behavior

Local labels such as Tier 0, Mission Critical, HBI, CUI, or Export Controlled should map to canonical properties and effective controls.

Agents should speak locally

Canonical requests can remain stable while agent responses, UI labels, reports, and workflows use the organization's preferred language.

Compliance & frameworks

Profiles, not product forks

Regulations, contractual requirements, management standards, and practice frameworks should be modeled distinctly, then mapped to internal controls and classifications.

Legal / regulatoryITAR, EAR, HIPAA, GDPR, SOX, privacy laws
Assurance / contractualCMMC, FedRAMP, SOC 2, PCI DSS
Management standardsISO 27001, ISO 42001, ISO 20000, ISO 22301
Practice frameworksNIST CSF, NIST SSDF, NIST AI RMF, ITIL, FinOps, SRE, OWASP, SLSA

Adoption principle

Speak locally. Govern consistently.

The family provides a governance grammar. The organization provides its own governance vocabulary.
