Evidence & Assurance

Proof

Evidence should explain what we know and why we trust it

Do not collapse source quality, model confidence, attestation, and independent assurance into one vague “trust score.” Preserve the dimensions separately.

Evidence basis

Declared, Observed, Identified, Inferred, Calculated, Verified.

How was the claim established?

Confidence

Primarily for inferred or probabilistic evidence. Preserve the producer’s uncertainty instead of disguising inference as fact.

How certain is the producer?

Assurance

A0 Unattested · A1 System Stamped · A2 Owner Attested · A3 Independent Validation · A4 Independent Assurance · A5 External Certification.

Who or what stands behind the claim?

Evidence objects

Proof has several different artifacts

Stamp

A compact machine-readable assertion, validation, attestation, or approval attached to evidence.

CoA

A generated point-in-time Certificate of Analysis summarizing conformance evidence for a Batch, release, artifact, or deployment.

Batch Record

The accumulated lifecycle record: builds, evaluations, deployments, changes, exceptions, incidents, remediation, and Proof references.

Genealogy

Traceability across source, dependencies, packages, models, agents, tools, artifacts, and deployments.

Roles

Validator, Notary, and Auditor are not the same thing

Validator“I tested the claim.”
Notary / Witness“I verified producer identity, signature, timestamp, integrity, and provenance.”
Auditor / Assurance“I independently assessed whether the evidence and controls are sufficient.”

Stamp policy

Inference may create a finding without being allowed to block.

A control can require a minimum evidence basis and assurance level. For example, inferred evidence may open an investigation, while an authoritative API verification may be required before blocking production.
